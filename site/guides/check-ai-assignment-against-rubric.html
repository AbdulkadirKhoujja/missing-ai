How to Check an AI Draft Against Assignment Criteria

A strong-sounding assignment draft can still miss a criterion. The safest way to review it is to compare each original rubric item with the text in the draft—and keep the final judgment with the student or assessor.

Start with the complete rubric

Use the actual assignment criteria, learning outcomes and submission instructions. Note formatting requirements, sources, word-count conditions, deadlines and academic-integrity rules. Do not treat a public generic rubric as a substitute for your own institution's instructions.

Make a rubric-to-draft checklist

For every criterion, record what passage in the draft might address it, what is merely implied, and what is not clearly mentioned. “Critical evaluation” may require more than naming advantages and disadvantages; a separate reference requirement might need evidence that citations were actually checked.

Distinguish content review from grading

Missing AI is not a grading service. It does not assign valid marks, certify a submission or guarantee academic success. The browser checker can highlight source lines that do not share terms with the pasted draft, but it can flag legitimate paraphrases. Use its findings as questions to investigate, not an assessment result.

Respect your course's AI rules

Some assignments restrict AI-generated writing, require disclosure, or prohibit submitting work to outside services. Always follow those rules. In the shared beta, use fictional or public sample material rather than identifiable student records or protected assignment documents.

Try a harmless sample

Rubric example: Define the concept; compare two approaches; cite two relevant sources; explain one limitation.

Draft answer: “The concept is defined below. Approach A and B have different costs. I have not added references or discussed limitations.”

Review: Source citations and limitations are acknowledged as missing; comparison quality remains uncertain. Do not infer a grade.

Useful follow-up questions

“Where is the evidence for criterion 3?” “Is a recommendation different from a mandatory requirement?” “What subpart of the rubric did I overlook?” Then make revisions yourself as allowed by the academic policy.
