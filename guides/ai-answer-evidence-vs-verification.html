AI Answer Evidence vs Actual Verification: What's the Difference?

An evidence-backed review should make it easier to see what an AI response claims. It should not turn those claims into facts without independent checks.

Evidence of a claim is not proof of a result

Imagine an AI writes, “Every payment has a receipt.” That sentence is evidence the AI claimed receipt support. It is not evidence that receipts are delivered, that a payment provider integration works, or that every relevant test passes.

Why exact quotes matter

An exact quotation and its position in the answer make the review traceable. Missing AI's host-assisted MCP validator checks that a supplied quote appears at the specified character position and that all extracted requirement IDs are accounted for. It cannot determine whether the quote genuinely satisfies a requirement.

Why semantic judgments vary

Two capable AI hosts may interpret a requirement differently, particularly for compound instructions, conditional guidance and future promises. In a public GOV.UK accessibility case study, ChatGPT and Claude produced different numbers of proposed groups. The difference is a prompt for careful adjudication, not evidence one host is universally more accurate.

What needs independent testing?

For code: review the relevant files, execute tests and inspect observed behaviour. For citations: verify the cited primary source supports the conclusion. For legal requirements: review applicability and seek qualified advice when appropriate. For accessibility: inspect the implementation and test with people and assistive technologies.

How to use the checker responsibly

Begin with a public or fictional brief. Run a local wording check, then examine the highlighted source lines yourself. For harder paraphrases you may use an AI-host semantic review if you understand its privacy trade-offs. Keep classifications such as “cannot determine” instead of forcing a yes/no answer.

One important difference

Quoted answer: “We have not commissioned an independent accessibility audit.” This does not establish whether a suitably qualified internal audit happened. Whether an audit meets a requirement depends on the original guidance and facts that the answer may not contain.
