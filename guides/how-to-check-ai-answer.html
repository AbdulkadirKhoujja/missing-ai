How to Check If an AI Answer Missed Your Instructions

An AI answer can sound complete while leaving part of the original request unresolved. A useful second check starts with the source requirements, not with asking the same AI whether it made a mistake.

1. Keep the full original brief

Save the instructions or public specification you actually gave the assistant. Separate distinct requirements, including small conditions such as timing, formats, exclusions, and what the output must include. A copied summary can lose the very details you need to check.

2. Compare the answer against each requirement

For every requirement, ask whether the AI answer explicitly addresses it, merely discusses a related topic, or says it has not been done. Treat promises such as “I will add it later” differently from claims of completed work. If the answer has no evidence, say cannot determine rather than declaring success or failure.

3. Look for partial coverage and paraphrases

One sentence can address part of a compound requirement. “We run automated tests” is not evidence of manual tests, and “we checked a screen reader” is not proof the entire service works with assistive technology. Conversely, a cancellation flow may be called “withdraw a booking.” A basic keyword checker may flag such paraphrases even when the claim is covered.

4. Check the original source, not just the report

Missing AI's free browser checker highlights wording-based candidates for review. It does not prove semantic omissions. Its ChatGPT/Claude custom-MCP beta can ask the host model to interpret source requirements and then validate exact answer quotes, but the host's interpretation still needs a human check.

5. Decide what to do next

Ask targeted follow-ups: “Show where the answer addresses requirement 4,” “Which acceptance test demonstrates the cancellation flow?” or “What is still not implemented?” If the outcome matters, inspect the real document, code, test or service separately. A statement of completion is not an implementation audit.

Worked fictional example

Brief: Verify payment webhook signatures; allow booking cancellation; issue payment receipts.

AI answer: “Stripe events are checked using the signing secret. Customers can withdraw bookings. Payment receipts have not been implemented.”

Review: Two capabilities are claimed; receipts are explicitly not implemented. None is independently verified. The web lexical checker may classify the paraphrased cancellation differently.
